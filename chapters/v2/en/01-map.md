# Chapter 1: The Director's First-Principles Map

Why does someone else's 15 seconds feel like a movie, and yours feels like a security camera? It's not the phone — it's the 7 meetings the other person held in their head before hitting "record," and you held none of them.

![Wall of storyboard cards, number 15 in the middle](../images/ch01-map.webp)

## Introduction: The Map

First, the foundation Book 1 left us, then what's new here.

**Photo = counting photons**: 1 moment, 8 decisions. Book 1 covered it. The 8 decisions govern "what each shot looks like."

**Video = 24 photos/second + sound + story**: two new things — **time** (shots in a sequence) and **sound**. This book's 7 principles govern "the relationships between shots":

1. **Film = time + sequence**: meaning lives in juxtaposition, not in a single shot.
2. **Shot = attention**: every shot answers "where should eyes go now?"
3. **Editing = omission**: don't film everything — the viewer's brain fills the gaps.
4. **Sound = half the movie**: dialogue, music, sound effects, silence.
5. **Story = change**: a character goes from state A to state B.
6. **Continuity = belief**: one world, and it must not collapse.
7. **Director = translator**: feelings → shot language → prompt.

**Director = photographer + time + sound + story.** The photographer decides "what this shot looks like"; the director decides "in what order the shots appear, how long each lasts, what sound goes with it, and what change it tells."

## The 7 Principles, One Line Each

- **Sequence**: what you show first, then what, and what you leave last — change the order, change the meaning.
- **Attention**: close-up magnifies emotion, wide shot explains relationships; push in is "look here," pull out is "take in the whole room."
- **Omission**: cut in the middle of an action and the cut disappears; film everything and you've said nothing.
- **Sound**: BGM sets the tone, sound effects set reality, silence sets tension; dialogue is always layer 1.
- **Change**: hook (first 3 seconds) → buildup → twist; no change, no story.
- **Belief**: eyelines match, direction doesn't flip, action connects, light doesn't jump — the world must hold.
- **Translation**: vague directions make the AI guess; precise directions make the AI the best crew you ever had.

## Try It on a 15-Second Short

Your 15 seconds is the smallest exam for all 7 principles: is the order right (Ch. 2), does attention have a landing spot (Ch. 3), did you cut the excess (Ch. 4), are the sound layers right (Ch. 5), is there a change (Ch. 6), does the world hold (Ch. 7), did the prompt say it clearly (Ch. 8)? Pass all 7, and the short is watchable to the end.

## Bridge: Translating It into an AI Prompt

A director's language = shot language. An AI video prompt = picture (8 decisions) + shot language (sequence / attention / duration / sound note):

- "Girl in a milk tea shop, blurred background" + "camera pushes in slowly" + "3 seconds" + "story job: hook"
- "Skateboarder mid-air" + "camera follows" + "2 seconds" + "story job: twist"

Formula: **picture + movement + duration + sound note + story job.** The story job is written for yourself: every shot knows why it's there.
