# Chapter 11: Bloopers — 12 Problems Fixed in One Place

**Map position**: Fix-it chapter · 6 principles + 6 hands-on.

A blooper isn't failure — it's "tuition." This chapter fixes the 12 most common problems in one place: each problem = symptom → cause → self-check → fix → prevention.

![Director's self-check list: 12 blooper problems, all marked for fixing](../images/ch10-bloopers.webp)

## Principles (6)

**1. Order scrambled — nobody knows what I'm saying**
Symptom: every shot looks good, together they mean nothing. Cause: shot without writing the order. Self-check: can I answer the three order questions? Fix: write the order first (hook → buildup → twist), then shoot. Prevention: write the order at the top of the shot list before rolling.

**2. The shot doesn't tell viewers where to look**
Symptom: too much in frame, eyes have nowhere to land. Cause: no landing spot set. Self-check: where do eyes go in this shot? Fix: subtract — keep one landing spot, blur or move the rest out of frame. Prevention: write the "landing spot" in the shot list.

**3. Filmed everything — 15 seconds of diary**
Symptom: no cutting, all footage. Cause: never deleted. Self-check: delete this shot — does the story still hold? Fix: cut until nothing can be cut. Prevention: make a "don't film" list before shooting.

**4. BGM buries the voice**
Symptom: can't hear the story. Cause: sound layers in the wrong order. Self-check: is the voice the loudest layer? Fix: drop the BGM 3 dB and listen again. Prevention: write the "sound note" in the shot list.

**5. 15 seconds that said nothing**
Symptom: no change, like a screensaver. Cause: no one-sentence story written. Self-check: did the character go from state A to state B? Fix: add a twist. Prevention: write the one-sentence story before rolling.

**6. Eyelines jump — viewers drop out**
Symptom: nobody knows who's looking at whom. Cause: eyeline, direction, action, light — one of the four lines broke. Self-check: do all four lines hold? Fix: add one "eyeline-establishing" shot. Prevention: write "anchor words" in the shot list.

## Hands-On (6)

**7. Vague prompt — AI guesses**
Symptom: the result isn't what you pictured. Cause: feeling words never got translated into shot language. Self-check: are there "feeling words" in the prompt? Fix: feeling → shot language → prompt, three steps of translation. Prevention: write the "feeling word" in the shot list.

**8. The AI person looks different at second 1 and second 10**
Symptom: consistency collapsed. Cause: anchor words incomplete. Self-check: are the anchor words identical in every prompt? Fix: character sheet + reference image + short segments. Prevention: write identical anchor words in every prompt.

**9. Reshoot rewrote the whole prompt — AI drifted again**
Symptom: the more you reshoot, the further it drifts. Cause: changed too much. Self-check: did I change only the wrong line? Fix: a reshoot changes only the wrong line. Prevention: keep the original prompt on file; change one line, log one line.

**10. First 3 seconds don't hold anyone**
Symptom: viewers scroll. Cause: the hook didn't land. Self-check: is the loudest shot the first one? Fix: move the loudest shot to shot 1 — loudest first, explanation after. Prevention: order question #1 — "what do I show first?"

**11. Sound finished, feels wrong**
Symptom: sound and picture are "two skins." Cause: no sound note written. Self-check: did every shot get a "sound note"? Fix: re-do the sound following the shot list's "sound notes." Prevention: write the "sound note" in the shot list.

**12. Assembled and the story doesn't close**
Symptom: a shot is missing, the story breaks. Cause: the shot list wasn't filled in. Self-check: are all 7 columns filled? Fix: reshoot the missing shot. Prevention: table not full, don't roll.
