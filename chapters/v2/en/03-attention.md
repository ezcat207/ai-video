# Chapter 3: Attention — Where Each Shot Tells Eyes to Go

**Map position**: Principle 2 · Shot = attention.

Too much in frame, eyes have nowhere to land — viewers scroll in 3 seconds. The director's second job: give the audience exactly one "where to look" per shot, and say it clearly.

![Teen pushing a stabilizer forward, tracking a girl on a skateboard](../images/ch02-movement.webp)

Look at this picture: a teen pushes a stabilizer forward, the camera tracks the girl on the skateboard, the background smears into motion blur. The eyes have exactly one place to go — the skateboarder. No matter how busy the background is, it can't steal attention. That's "one shot, one landing spot."

## What Attention Is

**One line**: the director decides for the viewer's eyes. Shot size is the zoom of attention; camera movement is the pull of attention.

**For a 13-year-old**:

- **Shot size is the zoom**: close-up = magnify emotion (face, eyes, hands); medium = tell the action (person + action); wide = explain relationships (where, with whom, doing what). Want viewers to *feel*? Close-up. Want them to *understand*? Wide.
- **Movement is the pull**: push in = "look here," dragging attention to a point; pull out = "take in the whole room," releasing attention; pan = "look around," guiding eyes on a tour; follow = "run with them," attention sticks to the moving person.
- **One shot, one landing spot**: the test — cover everything except one point. Does the story still hold? Yes, the shot passes. No, the frame is too full.

**Two cold facts**:
1. Attention needs a refill every 3 seconds: the same landing spot held too long and eyes start wandering — either cut, or move the camera.
2. Sound pulls attention too: a meow off-screen and eyes go looking for the cat — sound is an invisible pointing finger.

## Try It on a 15-Second Short

For your 15 seconds, set the landing spot for every shot first: this shot — where do eyes go? Write it in the shot list. No landing spot, no shoot — otherwise the frame comes out "eyes with nowhere to land."

## Prompt Lesson: Write the Landing Spot into the Prompt

**Example 1 (close-up, landing spot = the eyes)**:
- Chinese: 猫的脸部特写，眼睛睁大，背景虚化，镜头静止，2 秒，落点：眼睛
- English: Extreme close-up of a cat's face, eyes widening, blurred background, static camera, 2 seconds, focus point: the eyes

**Example 2 (follow, landing spot = the person)**:
- Chinese: 少年在操场上奔跑，镜头跟随他横向移动，背景动态模糊，2 秒，落点：奔跑的少年
- English: A teenage boy running on a track, camera trucks alongside him, motion-blurred background, 2 seconds, focus point: the running boy

**Key**: write the "focus point" in the prompt — it's a check for yourself: in this shot, where exactly do the eyes land?

## Experiment You Can Repeat

Shoot two 5-second clips on your phone: one with 3 things competing for attention, one with a single landing spot. Send them to a friend and ask "what did you notice first?" — one comparison and attention clicks.

## Blooper Warnings

- **Full-frame blooper**: people, signs, billboards, passersby all competing — cut: this shot needs one landing spot; blur or move the rest out of frame.
- **Drifting landing spot blooper**: the camera moved for half a minute and the landing spot changed 3 times — one shot, one landing spot; change the landing spot, cut the shot.
