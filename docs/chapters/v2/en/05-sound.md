# Chapter 5: Sound — Half the Movie

**Map position**: Principle 4 · Sound = half the movie.

Mute a horror movie and it's not scary at all. Sound sets emotion — the director's fourth job: give every shot a "sound layer order" — dialogue layer 1, sound effects layer 2, music layer 3, silence the most expensive of all.

![Teen cupping hands around a microphone, warm desk lamp](images/ch05-sound.webp)

Look at this picture: a teen recording at a microphone, warm lamp, a room so quiet you can hear breathing. Sound isn't "added later" — it's "planned while shooting." The best sound design is the kind viewers "don't notice" — they only feel "this picture was always supposed to sound like this."

## What Sound Is

**One line**: the layer order of sound. Dialogue layer 1, sound effects layer 2, music layer 3, silence layer 0 — the most expensive layer.

**For a 13-year-old**:

- **Dialogue layer 1**: the human voice always comes first. No matter how good the BGM is, it must not cover the voice — cover it and the audience "can't hear the story."
- **Sound effects layer 2**: footsteps, doors closing, a meow — effects set "real." A picture with no effects feels like a "silent film"; viewers sense "fake."
- **Music layer 3**: BGM sets the tone — tense, warm, funny. Music is the "background color of emotion," not the lead.
- **Silence is the most expensive**: suddenly no sound, and the audience holds its breath — silence is "the loudest sound effect." A half-beat of silence before the twist doubles the impact.

**Two cold facts**:
1. Sound can "pull attention": a meow off-screen and eyes go looking for the cat — sound is an invisible pointing finger.
2. AI video tools generate "picture"; sound usually gets added separately — the director writes the "sound note" in the shot list, then records voice, effects, and music after generation.

## Try It on a 15-Second Short

For your 15 seconds, write a "sound note" for every shot: what sound does this shot have? Dialogue, effects, music, or silence — write it down. Sound notes live in the shot list, so sound design isn't "figured out last minute."

## Prompt Lesson: Write the Sound Note into the Shot List

AI-generated picture has no sound — the sound note is written for yourself, to follow when you add sound.

**Example (sound notes)**:
- Shot 1: close-up of a cat's face, eyes widening, 2 seconds; sound: 1 second of silence, then a meow
- Shot 2: cat pouncing on a toy mouse, camera following, 3 seconds; sound: light BGM starts, effect: paws landing with a "thud"
- Shot 3: cat swatting the remote away, smugly looking at camera, 3 seconds; sound: BGM pauses half a beat, effect: the remote hitting the floor with a "clatter"

**Key**: write a "sound note" for every shot — dialogue, effects, music, or silence, pick one, write it clearly.

## Experiment You Can Repeat

Take one 5-second clip and give it two soundtracks: version A with tense BGM, version B with funny BGM. Send them to a friend and ask "what story do you think this is?" — one soundtrack swap and sound clicks.

## Blooper Warnings

- **Buried-voice blooper**: BGM covers the voice — check: is the voice always the loudest layer? Drop the BGM 3 dB and listen again.
- **Wall-to-wall blooper**: every shot has BGM, no silence anywhere — leave one silence; the twist needs the room to hit.
