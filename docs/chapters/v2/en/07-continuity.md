# Chapter 7: Belief — Continuity, One World

**Map position**: Principle 6 · Continuity = belief.

Eyelines jump around and viewers drop out. The director's sixth job: make the audience believe "this is one world" — eyelines match, direction doesn't flip, action connects, light doesn't jump. Break one line, and belief cracks a piece off.

![Teen glancing back over the shoulder at a door at the end of a hallway](images/ch07-consistency.webp)

Look at this picture: a teen glances back over the shoulder at a door at the end of a hallway. Viewers automatically know "he's looking at that door" — because eyeline, direction, and spatial relationships all line up. Continuity means "viewers never have to think to know who's looking at whom, where they are, and what just happened."

## What Belief Is

**One line**: continuity = one world. Eyeline, direction, action, light — four lines that must not break.

**For a 13-year-old**:

- **Eyelines match**: A looks at B, B looks at A — the eyeline directions have to match. A looks left, B has to look right — get it wrong and viewers don't know "who's looking at whom."
- **Direction doesn't flip**: the 180-degree axis — the line connecting two people is an axis, and the camera must not cross it. Cross it and the two people "swap sides" and viewers get dizzy.
- **Action connects**: last shot "hand raised halfway," next shot "hand keeps rising" — the action has to connect. Break it and viewers feel "a hitch."
- **Light doesn't jump**: same scene, light can't suddenly go from "dusk" to "noon" — light jumps and the world "became a different one."

**Two cold facts**:
1. The AI "consistency" problem lives right here: the AI generates one shot at a time, and the person at second 1 and second 10 "looks different" — that is "one world" collapsing. Chapter 10 treats it directly.
2. Continuity is "invisible": done right, viewers never feel it; done wrong, they drop out instantly — the best continuity is the kind you "can't feel at all."

## Try It on a 15-Second Short

For your 15 seconds, check the four lines: do the eyelines match? Did direction flip? Does the action connect? Did the light jump? All four pass, and the world stands.

## Prompt Lesson: Continuity Anchor Words

An AI generates one shot at a time — continuity depends on "anchor words": the same "character description + scene description + light description" in every prompt, so the AI can "remember" one world.

**Example (anchor words)**:
- Shot 1: orange tabby cat, green eyes, white collar, in a milk tea shop, warm yellow light, close-up of the cat's face, eyes widening, 2 seconds
- Shot 2: orange tabby cat, green eyes, white collar, in a milk tea shop, warm yellow light, cat sneaking up on a toy mouse, camera following, 3 seconds
- Shot 3: orange tabby cat, green eyes, white collar, in a milk tea shop, warm yellow light, cat swatting the remote away, smugly looking at camera, 3 seconds

**Key**: anchor words = "character + scene + light," identical in every prompt — that's how the AI "remembers" one world.

## Experiment You Can Repeat

Shoot two "conversations" on your phone: version A with matching eyelines (A looks left, B looks right), version B with mismatched eyelines (both look left). Send them to a friend and ask "who's talking to whom?" — one eyeline comparison and belief clicks.

## Blooper Warnings

- **Swapped-sides blooper**: the camera crossed the axis and the two people swapped sides — check: is the camera still on the same side of the line between them?
- **Changed-face blooper**: the AI person looks different at second 1 and second 10 — check: are the anchor words complete? Is the character description identical in every prompt?
