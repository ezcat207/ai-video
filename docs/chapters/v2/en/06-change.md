# Chapter 6: Change — The Smallest Unit of Story

**Map position**: Principle 5 · Story = change.

Fifteen seconds that said nothing — because nothing changed. The director's fifth job: move a character from state A to state B. No change, screensaver; change, story.

![Teen at two monitors: first screen is suspense and hook, second is twist and answer](images/ch06-story.webp)

Look at this picture: the left screen is "suspense and hook," the right screen is "twist and answer" — between the two screens is "change." Viewers finish the left screen wanting to know "then what?" and the right screen answers — that is the smallest unit of story.

## What Change Is

**One line**: story = a character going from state A to state B. The smallest story = hook (first 3 seconds) → buildup → twist.

**For a 13-year-old**:

- **The hook is the first 3 seconds**: in a 15-second short, if the first 3 seconds don't hold people, the other 12 are as good as unshot. Hook = "the loudest shot" or "the weirdest line" — show the loudest thing first, explain later.
- **Buildup is "then what?"**: the hook is thrown, viewers want to know "then what?" — buildup is the 3 shots that answer "then what?" Each shot answers it once, and viewers watch 3 more seconds.
- **The twist is "didn't see that coming"**: the ending has to be unexpected — the cat didn't catch the mouse, it caught the remote. The twist is "the peak of the change": from A to B, and B has to surprise.

**Two cold facts**:
1. Change can be small: "the cat went from 'wanting to catch' to 'caught it'" is a change; "the cat went from 'wanting to catch' to 'caught the remote'" is a bigger change — the bigger the twist, the better viewers remember it.
2. The one-sentence story: before shooting, write one sentence — "a cat wants to catch a mouse and ends up catching the remote." If one sentence can't say it, don't roll.

## Try It on a 15-Second Short

For your 15 seconds, write the one-sentence story first, then split it into 3 parts: hook (0–3s) → buildup (3–12s) → twist (12–15s). The one-sentence story goes at the top of the shot list, where you can see it while shooting.

## Prompt Lesson: Write the Story Job into the Prompt

Add one line at the end of every prompt: its "story job" — is this shot the hook, the buildup, or the twist?

**Example (story jobs)**:
- Shot 1: close-up of a cat's face, eyes widening, 2 seconds; story job: hook
- Shot 2: cat sneaking up on a toy mouse, camera following, 3 seconds; story job: buildup
- Shot 3: cat swatting the TV remote away, smugly looking at camera, 3 seconds; story job: twist

**Key**: every shot knows whether it's "hook / buildup / twist" — so generation never produces "a pile of shots with no idea which one matters."

## Experiment You Can Repeat

Take a 15-second clip you shot, cut the twist shot out, leave only hook and buildup. Send it to a friend and ask "how did that feel?" — one "deleted twist" and change clicks: without the twist, the story feels "unfinished."

## Blooper Warnings

- **Screensaver blooper**: 15 seconds with no change, like a screensaver — check: did the character go from state A to state B? No? Add a twist.
- **Hook blooper**: the first 3 seconds are flat and viewers scroll — move the loudest shot to shot 1; loudest first, explanation after.
