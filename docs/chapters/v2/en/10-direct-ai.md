# Chapter 10: Directing AI — From Shot List to Finished Film

**Map position**: Hands-on chapter · from shot list to finished film.

The AI person looks different at second 1 and second 10. The director's hands-on job: direct the AI one shot at a time — generate → check → reshoot → assemble. The AI is the crew, you are the director — when the crew has a problem, the director owns it.

![Teen at a desk late at night directing AI video generation, abstract video frame and progress bar on the laptop, storyboard sheet beside it](images/ch10-direct-ai.webp)

Look at this picture: a teen at a desk late at night, the laptop showing an AI-generated video frame and a progress bar, a shot list beside it. Directing isn't "press generate and you're done" — it's "generate → check → reshoot → assemble," round after round of grinding.

## What Directing AI Is

**One line**: the general workflow of ordinary AI video tools: shot list → one prompt per shot → generate → check → reshoot → assemble.

**For a 13-year-old**:

- **Text-to-video / image-to-video**: two ways to generate — "text-to-video" is "one sentence becomes a video," "image-to-video" is "one picture becomes a video." Image-to-video is steadier: first generate a "first frame" (the picture of the shot's first frame) with an image tool, then generate the video from that picture — the picture is an "anchor," so the shot is less likely to drift.
- **The first frame is the anchor**: for every shot, generate a "first frame" picture first; check it, then generate the video. First frame = "what this shot looks like"; video = "that look, moving."
- **Generate → check → reshoot**: after generating a shot, check 3 things: ① did the anchor words land (character, scene, light)? ② did the action land? ③ did the world hold? Failed? Change the prompt and generate again — that's a "reshoot."
- **Assemble**: once every shot is generated, stitch them in shot-list order, add sound (dialogue, effects, music, silence), add subtitles, export the film.

**Two cold facts**:
1. Consistency in practice: character sheet + reference image + short segments — a "character sheet" is a picture of "what the character looks like" (front, side, expressions), a "reference image" is a picture of "what the scene looks like," and "short segments" means "generate only 2–3 seconds at a time, then stitch." All three together is the steadiest consistency there is.
2. Reshoots are normal: directing is not "one generation and done" — good films are "generate 3 times, pick 1." Reshooting isn't embarrassing; reshooting is a director's daily life.

## Try It on a 15-Second Short

Your 15 seconds runs like this: shot list → generate a first-frame picture for each shot → check → generate the video → check → reshoot → assemble in order → add sound → export. Walk the whole flow and you have a finished film.

## Prompt Lesson: The Reshoot Prompt

A reshoot isn't "rewrite the whole thing" — it's "change only the line that was wrong":

**Original prompt**: Orange tabby cat, green eyes, white collar, in a milk tea shop, warm yellow light, close-up of the cat's face, eyes widening, static camera, 2 seconds

**Check**: the eyes didn't widen — it looks "asleep"

**Reshoot prompt**: Orange tabby cat, green eyes, white collar, in a milk tea shop, warm yellow light, close-up of the cat's face, **eyes suddenly widening, pupils dilating**, static camera, 2 seconds

**Key**: a reshoot changes only "the line that was wrong" — change more and the AI drifts again.

## Experiment You Can Repeat

Take one shot, generate it 3 times, pick the best. Compare the 3 results: which one held the "anchor words" steadiest? Which one got the "action" right? One "generate 3, pick 1" and directing AI clicks.

## Blooper Warnings

- **One-and-done blooper**: generating once and "settling" — good films are "generate 3, pick 1"; viewers can tell a settled film.
- **Rewrite-everything blooper**: changing the whole prompt on a reshoot and the AI drifts again — a reshoot changes only the wrong line.
