# Chapter 8: Translation — Turning Feelings into Shot Language

**Map position**: Principle 7 · Director = translator.

Vague prompt, AI guesses. The director's seventh job: translate the "feeling" in your head into "shot language," then into sentences that can direct an AI. Say it vaguely and the AI guesses; say it precisely and the AI is the best crew you ever had.

![Teen at a whiteboard: a feeling sketch on the left, storyboard frames on the right, a big arrow between them](images/ch08-translate.webp)

Look at this picture: a teen at a whiteboard, a sketch of a "tense" expression on the left, three storyboard frames on the right, a big arrow in the middle — feeling → shot language → storyboard. The director's job is "translation": turning "I feel tense" into "close-up + push in + quick cuts + low bass."

## What Translation Is

**One line**: feeling → shot language → prompt. Three steps of translation, none skippable.

**For a 13-year-old**:

- **Step 1: feeling words**: name the feeling first — "tense," "warm," "funny," "suspense." Feeling words are "for yourself"; they don't go in the prompt.
- **Step 2: shot language**: translate the feeling into shot language — "tense" = close-up + push in + quick cuts + low bass; "warm" = medium shot + slow push + warm light; "funny" = medium shot + static + sound effect.
- **Step 3: prompt**: write the shot language as a prompt — "close-up + push in + 2 seconds + low-bass note." Prompts are "for the AI": concrete, with numbers, with action.

**Feeling dictionary (6 common ones)**:
- Tense = close-up + push in + quick cuts + low bass
- Warm = medium shot + slow push + warm light + soft music
- Funny = medium shot + static + sound effect + pause
- Suspense = wide shot + slow pan + silence + sudden cut
- Moving = close-up + slow push + soft light + piano
- Epic = establishing shot + pull out + slow motion + low bass

## Try It on a 15-Second Short

For your 15 seconds, write the "feeling word" for every shot first, then translate it into "shot language," then write the prompt. Feeling words live in the shot list, so translation never gets stuck on "I know what I want but I can't say it."

## Prompt Lesson: A Complete Translation

**Feeling**: "the cat is about to do something bad — tense and funny"

**Translation**: tense = close-up + push in; funny = medium shot + static + sound effect

**Prompts**:
- Shot 1: close-up of a cat's face, eyes widening, camera slowly pushing in, 2 seconds, feeling: tense
- Shot 2: cat sneaking up on a toy mouse, static camera, sound effect: paws landing with a "thud," 3 seconds, feeling: funny
- Shot 3: cat swatting the remote away, smugly looking at camera, 3 seconds, feeling: twist

**Key**: write the "feeling" in the prompt — it's written for yourself: what feeling does this shot need?

## Experiment You Can Repeat

Take one feeling word ("tense") and write two prompts: version A just says "tense," version B says "close-up + push in + 2 seconds + low bass." Generate both and compare — one comparison and translation clicks: the more specific you are, the better the AI listens.

## Blooper Warnings

- **Feeling-word blooper**: the prompt only says "tense," "nice," "cinematic" — the AI doesn't understand feeling words; translate them into shot language first.
- **Too-long blooper**: a 200-word prompt and the AI can't find the point — one shot, one feeling, one prompt.
