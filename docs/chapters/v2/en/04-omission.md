# Chapter 4: Omission — The Subtraction of Editing

**Map position**: Principle 3 · Editing = omission.

Film everything and 15 seconds reads like a diary. The director's third job: cut. Cut down to the few key beats, and the viewer's brain fills in the middle by itself — that's "closure," the cheapest special effect in film history.

![Teen at two monitors, editing timeline on screen](images/ch03-timeline.webp)

Look at this picture: a teen at an editing timeline, footage library on the left screen, timeline on the right. Not one clip on the timeline is extra — every shot knows why it's there. Editing isn't "assembling," it's "deleting."

## What Omission Is

**One line**: don't film everything — film the few key beats; the viewer's brain fills in the middle.

**For a 13-year-old**:

- **Closure is the brain filling in**: Shot A is "cat crouches," shot B is "the toy mouse flies off" — the viewer automatically pictures "the cat pounced." The middle beat never needs filming; the brain films it for you.
- **Cut in the middle of an action**: cut while the action is halfway done and the cut is invisible — the eyes are tracking the action, with no attention left for the cut. Cut on stillness and the cut shows.
- **A "don't film" list**: before shooting, list what you won't film — the whole walk, the whole door opening, the whole elevator wait. Film only "the start" and "the end"; delete the middle.

**Two cold facts**:
1. Omission is trusting the audience: explaining everything is not trusting them; leaving a little for them to fill in makes them *more* invested — because they "took part."
2. The 15-second cut-down: shoot 30 seconds, delete half, delete half again — what's left is 15 seconds. Anything you can't delete is "can't bear to lose it," not "needed."

## Try It on a 15-Second Short

For your 15 seconds, cut after shooting: delete this shot — does the story still hold? Yes? Delete it. Cut until nothing can be cut — what's left is all "can't be removed."

## Prompt Lesson: Write Only the Key Frame

An AI generates one shot at a time — "omission" means: each prompt writes only the **key frame**, not the process.

**Example (key frame only)**:
- Chinese: 猫从沙发上跃起，扑向一只老鼠玩具，镜头跟随，2 秒，关键帧：跃起瞬间
- English: Cat leaping off a sofa toward a toy mouse, camera following, 2 seconds, key frame: the leap

**Key**: write the "key frame" in the prompt — this shot needs exactly one moment to land; the process is the viewer's brain's job.

## Experiment You Can Repeat

Shoot "opening a door and walking in" on your phone: version A films the whole thing (walk over, open door, walk in, close door); version B films only "hand touches the doorknob" and "the door closes behind them." Which feels more like a movie? One comparison and omission clicks.

## Blooper Warnings

- **Can't-let-go blooper**: every shot feels "I can't delete this" — delete it; if the story still holds, it goes.
- **Jump-cut blooper**: cut too much and the audience can't follow — check: is the deleted shot the *only* one explaining cause and effect? If yes, keep it.
